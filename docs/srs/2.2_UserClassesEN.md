# 2.2 User classes and characteristics

**Owner:** Jakub Piwko · **Updated on:** 12/11/2026 · **Status:** draft for review
**Sources:** actors in [requisitos/README.md](../requisitos/README.md), stakeholders in [10.1](10.1_StakeholdersRACIEN.md), sessions E-1 to E-8 ([12.3](12.3_DetalheDadosElicitacaoEN.md)), assumptions in [2.5](2.5_PressupostosDependenciasEN.md)

A **user class** is a group of people who use FAIR-AMAP in a similar way. User classes are a subset of the stakeholders listed in section 10.1: they include only the people who interact with the system themselves. FAIR Software Solutions as a company, host institutions, the AMAP network, researchers, the payment provider and the regulators are stakeholders but not user classes. Automatic actions of the system (notifications, calculations), shown as the actor "System" in section 3, are not a user class either.

One person can belong to more than one class. For example, a co-producer can also be part of AMAP management, and a producer who starts an AMAP holds both the manager and producer roles (E-8). Roles are assigned per AMAP (FR-06).

## 2.2.1 User classes

| User class | Who they are | Frequency of use | Main functions used | Technical expertise | Access level | Importance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Co-producer** | Consumer who subscribes to the products of one or more AMAPs. Up to about 200 per AMAP, typically up to about 50 (AS-06). | **Prospective co-producer** | Person who wants to join an AMAP and does not yet have an active membership. | Once or a few times, before joining | Public AMAP page and portal (FR-02), joining request and waiting list (FR-03) | Unknown. | **FAIR administrator** | Member of FAIR's staff who manages the platform. A small number of people. | Occasional: when an AMAP registers, for support, and when FAIR's charges are calculated | Check the documents of a new AMAP and authorise it (FR-01, E-8), roles at platform level (FR-06), FAIR's charges (FR-34), an AMAP leaving the platform (FR-10) | High | Platform level: AMAPs and their configuration. Access to members' personal data only as needed for these tasks (NFR-04, NFR-08). | Secondary |

**Favoured** classes are those whose needs drive the product. The scope objectives in section 1.3 are about reducing the manual work of producers and AMAP management and giving co-producers clear information. If the needs of a favoured class conflict with those of a secondary class, the favoured class takes priority. A producer who applies to an AMAP (FR-08) is treated as a producer, with the status "applicant" until approved (requisitos/README.md).

## 2.2.3 Personas

Personas are **fictional** people who represent a user class and make its needs and pain points concrete. The three personas below represent the favoured classes. They are based on what was said in the sessions (sources in brackets), but they do not describe any real person.

### Persona 1: Ana, co-producer

| | |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | 34 years old, works full time in Porto, member of an AMAP for two years. Walks to the pick-up point after work. |
| **Uses today** | The order form (Google Forms), the email copy of her order, a note on her phone where she copies the prices so she knows what she owes (E-3). |
| **Goals** | Know what she will collect on each date and how much it weighs, because she carries it home on foot; be reminded of delivery dates; hear about one-off surplus offers; find the AMAP's rules easily (E-3). |
| **Pain points** | Does not know exactly how much she owes each producer; has no way to tell anyone that she cannot come; once found her bag missing because someone took the wrong one (E-3). |
| **What FAIR-AMAP gives her** | Subscription and history in one place (FR-18, FR-21), basket contents with weight (FR-25), delivery reminders (FR-35), absence and collection confirmation (FR-27, FR-28), one amount per period (FR-31, FR-32). |

### Persona 2: Rui, producer

| | |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | 45 years old, runs an organic vegetable farm that supplies several AMAPs in Porto, with deliveries every week in alternating AMAPs (E-2). |
| **Uses today** | One Excel file per AMAP with co-producers, delivery dates and the monthly amounts; WhatsApp messages to send the basket contents every week (E-2). |
| **Goals** | Spend less time on opening and closing orders, confirming them and reminding people; know totals early enough to plan crops, which take 60 to 100 days; get paid on time (E-2). |
| **Pain points** | No single task is hard, but there are many of them; sometimes learns only the next day that a basket was not collected; does the payment calculations by hand (E-2). |
| **What FAIR-AMAP gives him** | Catalogue reused from the previous cycle (FR-11), production plan with totals (FR-23), basket contents published once for everyone (FR-25), collection confirmed by co-producers (FR-28), payment summary per producer (FR-33). |

### Persona 3: Marta, AMAP management

| | |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | 41 years old, co-producer and volunteer in her AMAP's working group, which handles new members and the calendar (E-1, E-3). |
| **Uses today** | Email and WhatsApp groups, the shared order form and spreadsheets (E-1, E-3). |
| **Goals** | Assess producer applications, usually with a farm visit; manage the waiting list when the AMAP is full; keep the AMAP's rules and documents up to date (E-8). |
| **Pain points** | Volunteer work in her free time; information spread across several tools; no record of each member's status (3.1.1). |
| **What FAIR-AMAP gives her** | Joining requests and waiting list (FR-03), producer applications with visit records (FR-08), AMAP rules set by configuration (FR-01, NFR-24), notices to all members (FR-36), payment overview (FR-33). |

**Open point:** AMAP management was heard only once, in a 30-minute session with the lecturer acting in that role (E-8), and no prospective co-producer or FAIR administrator was heard (section 10.1). The characteristics of these classes are the group's understanding and should be validated in a future iteration (section 10.4, L2).
