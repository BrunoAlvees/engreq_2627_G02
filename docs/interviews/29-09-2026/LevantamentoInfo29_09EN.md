# FAIR-AMAP - Information gathered in the 29/09 session

**Updated on:** 07/10/2026

This document gathers the information collected in the 29/09 session, held during the theoretical class, with the participation of Sofia Araújo, representative of BioGoods. The session consisted of a presentation of BioGoods and its relationship with different AMAP in Porto, followed by questions and answers.

The information was gathered by the group members during the session. Points the group is not sure about are marked "to be confirmed".

The information gathered here represents the group's current understanding and does not yet constitute a specification validated by the client. It is the account of a single producer, so it describes how the AMAP it works with operate, and not necessarily all AMAP/CSA.

## 1. Interviewed stakeholder

Sofia Araújo holds a degree in Chemical Engineering (FEUP), Environment branch, and is currently an environmental consultant and agricultural manager. In 2011, Sofia Araújo founded BioGoods, a company that produces and sells food under Organic Production Mode (MPB). A significant part of its production is sold through a network of AMAP in the city of Porto.

In the context of the project, Sofia Araújo represents the point of view of a **producer** who takes part in several AMAP and who, in practice, also handles much of their organisation.

## 2. BioGoods and the AMAP network

BioGoods started by selling eggs, later adding other products such as honey, and then potatoes, onions, pumpkins and other vegetables. Variety grew because consumers asked for more products, as long as they were always produced directly by BioGoods, without resale.

AMAP mentioned:

- **AMAP Baixa** (UPTEC, Porto), since 2020. BioGoods became this AMAP's main producer in 2024, when the previous producer stopped taking part.
- **AMAP at the Carolina Michaëlis Kindergarten**, since 2024.
- An AMAP linked to the Faculty of Sciences of the University of Porto (Campo Alegre) was also mentioned. Its name and date are unclear.

It was mentioned that AMAP may or may not have contracts. The AMAP in the network are not formal associations; an AMAP may or may not be set up as an association.

## 3. Operation described in the session

### 3.1. Current system

BioGoods uses **Excel** to organise the AMAP, where it records information and performs several calculations. There is one file per AMAP with the co-producers and delivery dates, and the calculations give the monthly amount each co-producer has to pay. It was said that, as the activity grows, management becomes increasingly complicated, hence the wish to automate it.

Communication with co-producers is done through **WhatsApp**: individual messages in some AMAP and groups in others.

When asked about the hardest process, the stakeholder answered that none is particularly hard. What weighs is the **number of tasks**. These include opening and closing orders, reading them, confirming them to co-producers and reminding them of delivery dates.

### 3.2. Single-producer and multi-producer AMAP

An AMAP can be **single-producer** or **multi-producer**. It was mentioned that the software should consider this flexibility. A small producer can also join another to supply the same AMAP.

The example of around 15 products delivered at each location was mentioned.

### 3.3. The basket

Usually, each AMAP has a base product, the **"farm basket"** (cabaz da quinta): a fortnightly basket of vegetables (and fruit) of about 4 kg. It includes roughly 8 to 10 varieties (to be confirmed).

The basket is the same for all co-producers. However, if someone is **allergic or intolerant** to a food, or simply does not like it, they can ask for it to be excluded and the product is **replaced** with another. The stakeholder said the software should allow these exceptions to be recorded person by person.

The basket composition is **not promised in advance**, because nature cannot be controlled. Fruit used to be sold separately and was later integrated into the basket. This change was well accepted by the co-producers.

### 3.4. Extra products

Besides the basket, there are **extra products** (for example, eggs), paid separately, per order, with a **discount for basket holders**.

### 3.5. Abundance and scarcity

The basket is usually larger in summer and smaller in winter, when there is less variety. The co-producer is guaranteed a certain quantity over the subscription period, and deliveries are balanced between periods of abundance and scarcity. The example of **13 deliveries per semester** was given.

Storage crops (sweet potato, onion, pumpkin) are used in winter to keep variety.

### 3.6. Schedule and deliveries

The AMAP operate in **alternating weeks**. There are four AMAP organised so that each receives fortnightly while the producer delivers every week. This arrangement makes it possible, for example, to move the fruit, which spoils if it is not delivered. Anyone who wants weekly deliveries can join two AMAP.

Deliveries are usually on **Tuesday**, but it must be possible to have another day. There have been AMAP with deliveries on different days (for example, Tuesday and Thursday).

Delivery is made **by the producer**, who also handles transport to the delivery point. Usually there is one point per AMAP, but there can be more than one.

### 3.7. Subscription and payments

Joining an AMAP is done through a **subscription for a defined period**. 6 months was mentioned as the usual period, 3 months as a duration used previously, and 1 year as practised in some AMAP abroad.

Payment is **monthly** and the amount does not change, even in months with fewer Tuesdays and therefore fewer deliveries. The example of **€32 per month** was given.

Currently, **each farm issues its own invoice**.

Short subscriptions make production planning harder. In the first year, about 40% of the co-producers did not continue. The stakeholder said that **pauses and withdrawals** are rare and handled case by case, by talking with the co-producer, but that the rule is to stay for the whole period.

The stakeholder mentioned that **September** is when harvest planning is done.

### 3.8. When the co-producer cannot collect the basket

The co-producer commits to receiving the basket. If they cannot (holidays, illness), to avoid wasting the products, they can give it to another person or family, donate it, or share it among the other co-producers. The example of raffling it among staff was also given. It can also go to an institution agreed by the group, and the basket can be collected by another person on the co-producer's behalf.

### 3.9. Organisation and participation

It was mentioned that there used to be an assembly where they assessed whether everything was fine, for example the quality of the products. However, it was also mentioned that AMAP Baixa holds annual assemblies, while the more recent AMAP decided not to have them. In those cases, the main contact is the management of the institution where the AMAP operates (for example, the school).

An AMAP can be born from a request: in the school's case, the management showed interest, an email was sent to the parents, a presentation/workshop was held and the basket was adjusted with those interested.

### 3.10. Multi-producer baskets

In Portugal it is **not common** for producers to join together to compose baskets, because there are few people and not enough scale. The example of larger AMAP abroad includes an entity that assembles baskets with products from several producers and several delivery points.

The stakeholder considered it would be **ideal** to be able to make multi-producer baskets and have an organised way of distributing them. This would include knowing who produces what, who delivers what and in which weeks, and possibly a central management and distribution entity. It was said explicitly that this is not what exists today, but what could exist.

## 4. Needs and ideas for the software mentioned

The following points were mentioned by the stakeholder as desirable. They do not yet have a defined priority:

- **Basket composition visible on the platform** before delivery, instead of being communicated every week by WhatsApp.
- **Per-co-producer exceptions and substitutions** (allergies, intolerances, preferences).
- **Configurable delivery day**, even though Tuesday is the usual one.
- **Flexibility between single-producer and multi-producer AMAP.**
- **Confirmation of basket collection**, done by the co-producer themselves, because sometimes the products are not there when they arrive.
- **Delivery date reminders** for co-producers.
- **Multi-producer baskets and organised distribution**, presented as an ideal scenario and not as an immediate need.

## 5. Information mentioned but not yet sufficiently certain

- **Subscription length:** 6 months, 1 year and, previously, 3 months were mentioned. It is still unclear which durations must be supported and whether there are minimum durations for new co-producers.
- **Basket weight and variety:** about 4 kg and 8 to 10 varieties; exact values must be confirmed.
- **Assemblies:** it is unclear whether assemblies stopped or whether AMAP Baixa still holds them. This must be confirmed.
- **Delivery balancing:** 13 deliveries per semester were mentioned, but it is unclear how balance between seasons is measured (weight, value or variety).
- **Extra products:** it is still unclear how they are ordered, how the discount is calculated and how they are paid.
- **Pauses and withdrawals:** handled case by case; there are no defined rules.

## 6. Points to clarify in future sessions

- Which documents are used in the process (the Excel file, invoices, membership forms)? Can we access an anonymised example?
- How is payment made (payment method, timing, who receives it in multi-producer AMAP)?
- How is the monthly subscription amount determined?
- How does a new co-producer join, and is there a limit on places or a waiting list?
- Who confirms deliveries on the producer's side, and what happens if a product is missing?
- What information should each participant (producer, co-producer, institution) be able to view and change?
