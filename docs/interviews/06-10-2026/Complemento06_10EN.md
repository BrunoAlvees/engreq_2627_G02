## 2. Additional information

### 2.1. Order form and deadlines
- There is an **order deadline** tied to the semester's first delivery. Example: form released at the end of June, orders until the 4th, first delivery on the 7th [02:38–02:58].
- The form starts with the AMAP's **general operating rules** [01:29–01:43].
- The stakeholder **keeps the copy of the form** received by email and even copies the price tables into a note, so she knows **what she ordered and how much she owes**. Otherwise she has to ask the producer [17:53–19:09].

### 2.2. Products and rules per producer
- **Bread:** there are types with **different price levels** and **sizes** (small/large), including **gluten-free**. Changes are made by private message and **must be sent by Sunday**, because the dough starts fermenting on Monday for Tuesday's delivery [07:20–09:21].
- **Mushrooms** (new producer): two types, usually fresh. On cold days the producer **confirms on Monday** whether there will be any [10:33–10:58]. **Dried mushrooms** are possible as an alternative, **but they are not in the form** [09:28–10:21, 44:43–46:05].
- **Quinta da Serrinha** (olive oil): occasional deliveries (quarterly or per semester, to be confirmed), **handed over in person**; the producer has waited for and phoned co-producers who did not show up [30:01–30:37, 37:12].
- **Baskets:** there are several types (fruit only, farm basket and another one, to be confirmed), some with more vegetables and others with more fruit [04:54–05:54, 54:54]. As understood by the group, a combination of two baskets costs around **€50 per month** (the farm basket alone costs €32, according to the 29/09 session). **To be confirmed.**
- Separate products (e.g. **beans**) are ordered by **private message** and paid separately [53:34–54:13].

### 2.3. Collection
- **Previously**, products were left in bulk with a **list on the wall** and each co-producer put together their own basket. As many did not show up, producers were left waiting [34:20–35:05].
- **Now** producers deliver **pre-packed bags labelled with the co-producer's name** (e.g. "Name 1 of 2", "2 of 2") [35:05–36:11]. According to the stakeholder, this made the AMAP "a little less human" (discussed at the General Assembly on 05/10), but it made the process viable for producers [36:33–36:56].
- **There is no collection confirmation.** The space is not supervised. Baskets have been left until the next day, expensive products left in the heat, and someone else's bread taken [39:44–41:50]. When someone else collects on a co-producer's behalf, there is also no way to confirm it was the right person [43:24–44:20]. The stakeholder considers confirmation **useful for everyone**, especially if the AMAP grows [41:52–42:25].
- Not all producers are present at deliveries. Some run **routes across several delivery points** and used to wait up to two hours to hand over about 30 baskets [37:12–39:05].

### 2.4. Information the co-producer would like to have
- Knowing **what she will collect and how heavy it is**, because she walks (e.g. olive oil and nuts can add up to several kilos) [28:15–28:38].
- **Delivery reminders**: some people forget [29:57].
- **Occasional surplus offers** (e.g. heirloom tomatoes) that today only arrive by private message, and **quantity management** by producers [47:34–49:35].
- **Easy access to the AMAP's rules**: there are documents and an AMAP email address, but they are hard to find [50:23–51:10].

### 2.5. Basket exclusions
- Handled **informally**: the co-producer tells the producer what she doesn't like and the producer swaps it. The producer also knows each person's preferences [55:01–56:14].
